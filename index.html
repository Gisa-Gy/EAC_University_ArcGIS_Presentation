01Why geospatial technology matters

From Geographic Information to Intelligent Decision-Making

What is GIS?

A Geographic Information System is a system for collecting, managing, analysing, visualising and communicating information that is referenced to locations on the Earth.

What makes geographic information different
  • Position. Every record carries a geometry — a point, line, polygon or image cell — in a defined coordinate system.
  • Relationships. Distance, adjacency, containment and connectivity can be measured rather than assumed.
  • Integration. Data from different sources can be overlaid because they share location.
  • Scale. Patterns change with scale and resolution, so the analysis must match the question.
GIS is not simply a mapping tool. The map is usually the communication product at the end of a longer analytical workflow.
